Tab 01 • Empirical Threat Directory

Top 4 Social Media Scams Targeting Sri Lankan Youth

Empirical data from our Faculty of Engineering survey (N = 420 youth respondents) demonstrates that these 4 attack vectors account for over 84% of financial fraud and social media compromises in Sri Lanka.

Most Common (42% of Cases) WhatsApp / Telegram

1. “Like Videos & Earn” Part-Time Job Scams

Scammers message you offering Rs. 5,000–25,000/day to like YouTube or TikTok videos. They actually pay Rs. 1,000–1,500 at first to win your trust, then ask you to deposit money for “VIP Prepaid Tasks” and vanish with your savings.

Typical Message: “Earn Rs. 12,000 daily from your phone! Like 3 YouTube videos and message our Telegram receptionist for instant Rs. 1,500 bonus.”
✓ Safety Rule: Legitimate jobs never ask you to deposit money to unlock tasks or commissions.
High Risk (29% of Cases) SMS / FB / Instagram

2. Fake SL Post Parcel & Brand Giveaway Links

Fake SMS messages or social ads claim your Sri Lanka Post parcel is held for a small fee (e.g., Rs. 98) or that you won a Dialog/Mobitel/Daraz prize. The link leads to a fake website ending in .top or .xyz that steals your card details.

Typical Message: “SL-POST: Your parcel is held due to an incomplete address. Pay Rs. 98 fee within 2 hours at slpost-track.top.”
✓ Safety Rule: Check the website address carefully. Official Sri Lankan sites end in .gov.lk, .ac.lk, or .lk.
Account Hijacking (18% of Cases) WhatsApp / Instagram DM

3. 6-Digit OTP Code & Hacked Friend Messages

A friend’s already-hacked account messages you in Singlish/Sinhala asking for a 6-digit SMS code “sent to your phone by mistake” or asks for urgent hospital money via EzCash/bank transfer.

Typical Message: “Malli hadissi! I sent a 6-digit code to your number by mistake, ikmanata eka ewannako.”
✓ Safety Rule: Never share a 6-digit SMS OTP code. Always make a normal phone call to verify if a friend asks for money.
Growing Threat (11% of Cases) TikTok / Telegram / FB

4. Fake Crypto/Forex Doublers & Scholarship Scams

Pages impersonating trading gurus, influencers, or foreign scholarship agencies promise guaranteed 300% crypto returns or visa placements if you pay an upfront “processing or registration fee”.

Typical Message: “Invest USDT $50 in our automated AI pool and receive $250 back to your Binance wallet in 4 hours.”
✓ Safety Rule: Any scheme promising guaranteed fast returns or asking for upfront visa fees via personal accounts is a scam.

Development Programme Mini Project Report Summary

Project Title
Online Scam Awareness Among Sri Lankan Youth: Survey Analysis & Preventive Web Platform
Project Supervisor: Mr. B.D.S.M. Kavisigamuwa
Group Members: K.M.R.I. Suraweera (Developer), K.H. Sumuditha, S.D.T.A. Sumedhaarachchi, S. Thaaniyaluxman
K.M.R.I. Suraweera (Developer) • K.H. Sumuditha • S.D.T.A. Sumedhaarachchi • S. Thaaniyaluxman

1. Executive Abstract

With high social media penetration among Sri Lankan youth aged 16–26, scammers increasingly use WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, and TikTok to run “part-time task” Ponzi schemes, phishing giveaways, and OTP account takeovers. Our Development Programme Mini Project survey (N = 420 respondents) found that while youth use social platforms daily, 68.4% struggle to spot spoofed website domains (.top, .xyz) on mobile phones and 81.0% are unaware of official reporting procedures through Sri Lanka CERT|CC.

2. Key Survey Takeaways (N = 420 Youth Respondents)

  • 73.1% received unsolicited WhatsApp or Telegram messages offering high daily payouts for liking social media posts.
  • 42.0% of reported financial losses originated from “VIP Prepaid Task” traps after scammers paid a small initial bonus (Rs. 1,000–1,500) to build trust.
  • 68.4% could not reliably distinguish fake courier/telecom phishing links from official .lk websites.

🚨 Emergency Assistance • Sri Lanka

Where to Report Scams in Sri Lanka

If you or someone you know has shared a 6-digit OTP, clicked a phishing link, or transferred money to a scammer, contact these official organizations immediately:

  • Sri Lanka CERT|CC (National Cyber Security Center) Hotline: +94 11 269 1692 • Email: report@cert.gov.lk
  • CID Cyber Crime Division (Sri Lanka Police) Tel: 011-2422176 • Police Emergency: 119
    24/7 Police
  • Your Bank’s 24/7 Fraud & Card Hotline Call the phone number printed on the back of your ATM/Debit card immediately to freeze transfers or compromised accounts.
    Urgent
💡 Quick Action Advice: Take screenshots of suspicious WhatsApp/Telegram messages, transaction receipts, and phone numbers before blocking the scammer. Sri Lanka CERT and Police CID require these records when filing a formal cyber complaint.
